# What it costs

Captured 2026-09-07 MDT. All figures CAD unless noted.

## Baseline, for an average-sized pilot

Canadian sources put a complete new PPG package at **$10,000–$15,000**:

| Item | Range |
| --- | --- |
| Paramotor (engine + frame + harness) | $7,000–$11,000 |
| Wing | $3,000–$5,000 |
| Training | $2,500–$4,000 |
| **Total to flying** | **$12,500–$19,000** |

## The size premium

Every constraint in `sizing.md` pushes you to the expensive end, and two of them add
line items that an average pilot does not have:

| Item | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Biggest-class engine (Thor 260/303, Tornado 280) | Top of the motor range, not the middle |
| Top-size wing (31–34 m²) | Top of the wing range; more cloth costs more |
| **Tandem-rated reserve (180–220 kg)** | Extra cost over a standard reserve, and non-negotiable |
| **Made-to-measure harness** | Extra cost and extra lead time over off-the-shelf |
| Registration + CAR 606.02 liability insurance | Annual, ongoing |

**Realistic all-in: $16,000–$21,000** to be legally airborne on equipment that
actually fits, before fuel and before a trailer.

If the answer turns out to be a trike, add to that rather than substitute — trikes
cost more than foot-launch units and you still need somewhere to put it.

## Ongoing

- Fuel — premium pump gas plus two-stroke oil. Cheap per hour; this is not the
  expense.
- **Reserve repacks** on the manufacturer's schedule. Budget for it and actually do
  it.
- Wing life. A wing is a wear item — porosity and line trim degrade with hours and
  UV. See the used-gear note below for why this matters more for you than for most.
- Annual insurance and registration.
- Props. You will break one. Everyone breaks one.

## Buying used

The usual advice is "buy a used motor, buy a new wing." That advice holds here, and
harder.

**The motor, used: fine.** Engines and frames are inspectable, and a well-kept
two-stroke with known hours is a reasonable buy — *provided* the frame's published
pilot-weight rating covers you, which is exactly the thing most listings do not
state. Ask for the model and look up the manufacturer's number yourself.

**The wing, used: be careful.** A wing degrades with hours and sun. A tired wing has
higher porosity, worse launch behaviour, and less margin. You will be flying at or
near the **top** of your wing's certified range, which means you are already spending
the margin the certification allows. Spending it twice — top of range *and* a
300-hour wing — is the wrong trade. Buy new, or buy genuinely low-hour with a
porosity test.

**The harness: don't.** It has to be built to your measurements. That is the whole
point.

**The reserve: only with full history**, and only if it is genuinely rated to
180 kg+. Repack history, age, and no water exposure. This is the component whose
one job is the worst day.

## The lead-time trap

Most of what you need is a special order rather than stock: top-size wings,
big-class engines, tandem reserves, custom harnesses. Combine that with **Paramotor
Sports Canada being fully booked for 2026** and a 2–4 month weather-dependent
training window, and the sequencing matters more than the shopping.

Sensible order of operations:

1. **Waitlist application to a school now.** It costs nothing and it is the long
   pole.
2. **Ask the school the fit question** (`canada.md`) — their answer may change the
   equipment conversation entirely.
3. **Get frame pilot-weight ratings in writing** from two or three manufacturers.
4. Only then commit money to gear.

Buying a machine before step 2 is how people end up with an expensive garage
ornament.
