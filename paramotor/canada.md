# Flying legally in Alberta

Captured 2026-09-07 MDT. Regulatory details need confirming against Transport Canada
directly before acting — see `verify.md`.

## The short version

Canada is stricter than the United States about this. In the US, powered
paragliding falls under FAR Part 103 and needs no licence at all. **In Canada it
does not.** A PPG is classified as an **ultralight aeroplane**, which means:

1. You need a **Transport Canada Ultra-light Pilot Permit** (a Recreational Pilot
   Permit or full Pilot Licence also covers it).
2. The **aircraft must be registered** with Transport Canada.
3. You must carry **liability insurance of at least $100,000** (CAR 606.02).
4. **No passengers.** Training flights are the only exception.

Anyone telling you paramotors are unregulated in Canada is describing the US.

## Getting the permit

The path, roughly:

- **Training at a certified school.** Ground school plus flight training. The
  practical requirement is on the order of **5 hours of flight time and 30 solo
  flights**.
- **Written exam.** Transport Canada's **80-question** ultralight exam.
- **Medical.** A self-declaration category rather than an aviation medical for the
  ultralight permit — confirm the current requirement with TC, since this is the
  detail most likely to have changed.

Compressed courses run 6–10 days of actual instruction. In Alberta that does not
translate to 6–10 calendar days: schools here schedule around weather, and the
realistic figure quoted locally is **2–4 months start to finish**. Plan your season
accordingly — you want spring or early summer, not August.

## Registration and insurance

- Submit an *Application for Registration of Ultra-light or Advanced Ultra-light
  Aeroplanes* to Transport Canada, with a document certifying title to the aircraft.
- Liability insurance to the CAR 606.02 minimum. Get quotes early; it is a small
  market and the premium is not the interesting part — availability is.

## Schools in Alberta

**Paramotor Sports Canada** — Innisfail Airport, west of Innisfail, south-central
Alberta. Transport Canada Certified Flight Training Unit **#5191**. The obvious
first call: they are certified, they are close, and they are the ones who can tell
you whether their training equipment fits you.

⚠️ **Their 2026 season is already fully booked.** There is a waitlist via an online
application. Given the 2–4 month weather-dependent timeline, this realistically
makes 2027 the training year unless a spot opens — which makes the waitlist
application something to do now rather than after the gear research is finished.

**Air Adventure Flight School** — Alberta, worth calling as an alternative.

Wider afield, **Powered Paragliding Ontario** and **paragliding.ca** run PPG
training and publish useful material on the Transport Canada process even if you do
not train with them.

## The question to ask a school first

Not "when can I start" — **"what is the pilot weight and height limit on your
training equipment?"**

Training happens on the school's machines and the school's wings. If their fleet is
rated to 120 kg pilots, as much of the market is, then the equipment conversation in
`gear.md` is not the first problem — the training conversation is. It is entirely
possible that a school needs to source a specific wing or machine before they can
take you, and that is a lead-time question you want answered in the first phone
call, not the fifth.

Ask it early and ask it directly. A good school will give you a straight answer; it
is not an unusual question.

## Where you can actually fly

Ultralight rules constrain airspace, and Alberta has plenty of it around Calgary and
Edmonton. Not researched yet — this needs its own pass, covering controlled airspace
around the major terminals, rules over built-up areas, and what landowner permission
looks like for launching off private land. The school will know the local picture
better than any document.
