# Gear that actually fits

Captured 2026-09-07 MDT. Everything here is filtered against the ~170 kg all-up
weight and 203 cm height established in `sizing.md`.

Read the caveat in `verify.md` first: some spec tables could not be retrieved
directly and rest on secondary sources.

## Engines

Target from `sizing.md`: **110–120 kg static thrust at sea level**, so that
~90–95 kg survives an Alberta summer afternoon.

| Engine | Thrust (manufacturer) | Prop | Verdict for this pilot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vittorazi Moster 185 Plus | 75 kg @ 125 cm, 78 kg @ 130 cm | 125–130 cm | **Not enough.** The default choice for most pilots, and the wrong one here. |
| Air Conception Tornado 280 | 92 kg | 140 cm 3-blade | **Marginal but real.** Explicitly marketed for heavy and tandem pilots. Good power-to-weight. Would be tight on a hot day. |
| Polini Thor 260 | 105 kg | 160 cm | **Good.** Comfortably in range. |
| Polini Thor 303 | 120 kg | 160 cm | **The comfortable answer.** Replaced the Thor 250 in Polini's line. Heavy, big cage, usually paired with trikes and experienced pilots. |
| Simonini Mini 3 | 33 hp @ 7,000 rpm (thrust not published in sources found) | — | Worth pricing; the power class is right. |

Note the conflict: one secondary comparison site quotes the Moster 185 at 92 kg
with a 140 cm prop, against Vittorazi's own 75–78 kg figure. Trust the
manufacturer. Even at the optimistic number the Moster is at the bottom of what
you need before density altitude takes its cut.

**The 160 cm prop is its own consideration.** A bigger prop needs a bigger cage,
and a bigger cage is heavier, more awkward on the ground, harder to fit in a
vehicle, and more of a handful when you are kiting a wing in wind. It also raises
the assembled height, which interacts with a 203 cm pilot's frame geometry. Ask to
handle one before committing — this is the difference between a machine you fly
weekly and one you leave in the garage.

## Frames

The binding number is **published maximum pilot weight**, not total weight.

Confirmed exclusion:

- **Parajet Maverick / Maverick MAX** — published max pilot weight **265 lb
  (120 kg)**. You are over it. The MAX is the one marketed for "experienced, tandem
  and large pilots" and it carries the same limit.

The 120 kg figure recurs across the industry, so treat it as the default answer
until a manufacturer says otherwise in writing.

Worth asking directly, per model, in writing:

- **Air Conception** — Tornado 280 with the titanium Race V2 frame; the engine is
  aimed at heavy/tandem use, so the frame rating question is a fair one to put to
  them.
- **Fresh Breeze** (Germany) — long history of both foot-launch units and trikes,
  and a range built around different pilot weights.
- **Fly Products** — Eclipse with the Polini Thor is repeatedly named in
  heavy-pilot discussions.
- **Scout**, **Nirvana**, **Macfly** — Macfly builds around the Polini Thor 190–250
  range specifically.

The question to send is not "can I fly this?" It is: *"What is the published maximum
pilot weight for this frame, and will you confirm in writing that it covers a 129 kg
pilot at 203 cm with a [engine] and a [X] kg all-up weight?"* A dealer who will not
answer that in writing has answered it.

## Wings

Target from `sizing.md`: **31–34 m² flat**, which is the top size or beyond in most
ranges.

The worked example, and a genuinely good candidate:

- **Dudek Universal 1.1, size 34** — 34.00 m² flat, 29.31 m² projected.
  **EN range 135–170 kg. DGAC (ultralight) range 135–215 kg.** At ~170 kg you are
  exactly at the top of its EN band and mid-range on DGAC. Ships **without a speed
  system**, because Dudek intends this size for trikes. EN-B in the smaller sizes;
  size 34 carries ULM approval rather than EN/LTF B certification.

That last detail is the whole market in miniature: the size you need is the size the
manufacturer built for wheels.

Others to price in their largest sizes:

- **Dudek Nucleon** — reflex, sources put the top of its range at 170 kg.
- **Gin Falcon** — advanced reflex, DGAC certified.
- **APCO** — APCO publishes extended DGAC ranges above their EN ranges on several
  wings, which is exactly the mechanism you need; the Thrust line is their
  long-running beginner/intermediate wing.
- **Ozone Mojo PWR 2** — EN-A, the standard beginner recommendation. Check whether
  its largest size reaches you at all; if it does not, that is informative about
  where the beginner-wing market stops.
- **Tandem wings flown solo** — the traditional heavy-pilot fallback. Certified well
  above your weight, but slow, physically demanding to launch alone, and not a
  training wing.

**Reflex vs. non-reflex.** Reflex wings are the norm in PPG and are more stable at
speed, but they are generally not the wing you learn on. Expect a school to start
you on something docile, then move you up. Given that your size forces you toward
the top of every range, biasing toward the *most* forgiving wing that fits is the
right instinct, not the sportiest one that technically covers you.

## Reserve parachute

Suspended weight is the full ~170 kg. This rules out:

- Standard solo reserves (roughly 100–120 kg certified).
- Heavy solo reserves such as the **APCO Mayday Square 140** — rated 80–140 kg, with
  its quoted sub-5.5 m/s descent rate measured *at* 140 kg. Thirty kilos over that.

**You need a tandem-class or heavy-rated reserve, typically 180–220 kg certified.**
Square and semi-square designs are worth the money over round ones — better
pendulum stability and lower sink for the same area. Budget for the size and check
it physically fits the harness container, because a tandem reserve is bulkier than
whatever the container was designed around.

Then have it repacked on the manufacturer's schedule. A reserve at the top of its
range that has sat packed for three years is two problems, not one.

## Harness

Off-the-shelf XL sizing tops out around **2 m tall and 120 kg** (APCO's published
guidance is representative). You are over on both axes at once.

Plan on **made-to-measure**, ordered against real body measurements — torso length,
seated hip-to-knee, shoulder width, and your actual weight. What you are buying is
correct hang-point geometry and a seat that catches you in the right place; see
`sizing.md` for why those two things matter more than comfort.

Some harnesses offer adjustable hang points and a back-extension zip to stretch
across a size range. Useful, but a range extension on an XL is not the same as a
harness built for 203 cm. Ask what the actual maximum is, in centimetres.

**Nothing gets bought before a physical fitting.** No table substitutes for sitting
in the thing. If a dealer cannot arrange that, they are the wrong dealer.

## Trikes and quads

If the foot-launch route stalls on frame ratings or fitting, this is where it goes.

- Commonly rated **350–400 lb and up**, which clears your weight with margin.
- Removes the running launch entirely — no joint load, no forward-lean prop
  clearance problem, no wrestling 40 kg on your back across a field.
- Pairs naturally with the big trike-oriented wings (the Universal 34 problem
  becomes the Universal 34 solution).
- Costs more, weighs more, needs a decent surface to launch and land on, and needs
  a trailer or a truck.

**BlackHawk Lite Trike** and **Fresh Breeze**'s trike range are the obvious starting
points to price.
